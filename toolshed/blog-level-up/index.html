LVL1

Plan

Find your reason

Before any setup

You do not need a niche, a brand, or a logo. You need one honest reason to write and a rhythm you could keep on a bad week.

Level-up check You can say what the blog is, who it is for, and what your first three posts are.
LVL2

Plan

Claim your corner

Own the keys

The first blogging skill is not picking a theme. It is knowing which account owns the name, the site, the recovery email, and your posts.

Questions to ask before you pick a platform
  1. Can I export all my posts and images if I leave?
  2. Who owns the domain name, me or the platform?
  3. What happens to the blog if I stop paying or stop logging in?
  4. What are the current limits on themes, add-ons, storage, and custom code?
Level-up check You can name who controls the domain, the site account, the recovery path, and where your decisions are written.
LVL3

Publish

Publish your first post

Real words, real URL

A short post that exists beats a perfect post that does not. Draft it plainly, learn the editor by using it, then put it where people can read it.

What a post is made of
  • A title that says what the post is about.
  • An opening line that tells a reader why to stay.
  • Headings only where the post is long enough to need them.
  • Images with alt text, which is a short description for people who cannot see them.
  • A closing line, and a date.
Level-up check One real post is live, readable on a phone, and you have seen it the way a visitor does.
LVL4

Publish

Build the archive

Make old posts findable

Categories are big doors. Tags are small cross-links. The goal is a blog a stranger can browse in two clicks, and a post routine you can repeat without thinking.

A starter category shape
essays
how-to and notes
projects
life and updates
links and finds

Rename these to fit your voice. Choose categories you will still use after fifty more posts, and keep the set small enough to remember.

Level-up check A visitor understands the blog's purpose quickly and can browse older writing by subject.
LVL5

Grow

Be findable and welcoming

The public pass

The editor is not the website. Look at your blog the way a stranger would, with a private window, a phone, a keyboard, and ordinary patience.

Level-up check Shared links make sense, the site works with a keyboard and at higher zoom, and readers can reach you.
LVL6

Grow

Keep it alive

The long game

Blogs fail from neglect more often than from bad writing. A small care routine and a way back keep the whole thing standing.

Before any update, run this tiny ritual
  1. Confirm you have a current backup and know where it lives.
  2. Check where the update comes from and what it changes.
  3. Change one thing at a time.
  4. Test the public site and the admin side afterward.
Level-up check You can recover the site, update it safely, and come back to it next month without dread.

Boss checks

Before you call it live.

This is not about becoming a security expert overnight. It is about finding the predictable breakage before a visitor does.

Parked · not yet

Useful later. Not a way to dodge the basics.

Skip these for now

  • Switching platforms for the sake of switching
  • Paid upgrades before you can name what you need
  • Hunting for add-ons and plugins
  • Custom code, child themes, or a full redesign
  • Re-sorting every old post in one session
  • Watching traffic numbers every day

Unlock conditions

  • A specific missing capability requires it.
  • You can name the exact pain point, not just that it feels limited.
  • The navigation, archive, post routine, and public tests have shipped.

The rules

Four guard rails.

A blog should add to your life, not take it over. These keep it small enough to survive.

  1. Never hijack your main priority. If a job, class, or deadline needs you, this waits. The blog is a side quest until you decide otherwise.
  2. Keep a minimum heartbeat. One blog interaction a week: publish, edit, restructure, or tidy a template. A missed week means do the smallest one next, not a catch-up marathon.
  3. Ship an artifact every level. A live post, a menu, a page, a note. Research tabs alone do not count.
  4. One action per work block. Pick one change, finish it, test it, write it down. The sidebar does not need to arrive fully styled on day one.
